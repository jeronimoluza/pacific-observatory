How expensive is food, country by country?

Matched-basket price level in US$. The world median country = 100.
Each country's food prices are compared with the world median item by item — 1 kg of rice against 1 kg of rice, 1 litre of milk against 1 litre of milk — and then averaged. A country at 150 pays roughly 50% more in US dollars for the same shopping basket than the typical country here. Click any bar to open that country.
Jevons index: exp( medianleaves ln( Pc,leaf,unit / Pworld,leaf,unit ) ) × 100, over COICOP leaf × unit cells with n ≥ 3 observations, retail sources only, unflagged. Countries need + matched leaves, ≥2 sources and <20% flagged cells to be ranked. There are no expenditure weights — this is a price level, not a cost-of-living index.

Relative price divides each country's unit value by that country's own matched-basket level, so a common shift in the exchange rate cancels out. Above 1.00 = this item is dear relative to the local food basket. This is the FX-invariant comparison; the absolute view is not.

Price over time — and how much of it is the exchange rate

Indexed to the first period with data = 100.
When a price in US dollars rises, two different things can be happening: the shop really raised its price, or the local currency lost value against the dollar. The local currency line is the shop; the exchange rate line is the currency. The US$ line is what the two do together.
Exact identity in logs: Δln PUSD = Δln Plocal − Δln FX, where FX is local currency per US$. The decomposition below splits the window's total US$ move into the local-price part and the FX part; they add up exactly.
